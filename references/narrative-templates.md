# Narrative templates

Pick one template, then adapt it. These come from four reference decks that all follow the same logic: **the deck answers the prompt's sub-questions in the order they were asked, and the section tracker mirrors those sub-questions.** A reader who sees only the tracker labels and the takeaway strips should be able to retell the story.

## Choosing a template

| The prompt sounds like | Use |
|---|---|
| "Design a product that…", "Build a feature for…", "Improve X for Y" | A: Product build |
| "How would you launch / grow / go to market…", "You are an agent/founder who wants…" | B: Strategy / GTM |
| "Should we enter this market?", "Evaluate quick commerce for students", research-led case | C: Market and unit economics |
| "How would you colonise / roll out / transform… over N years", multi-stage programs | D: Phased roadmap |
| Teardown of an existing product | A, with "Problem" replaced by "What it does today" and a "Gaps and opportunities" slide before "Solution" |
| PRD or launch walkthrough | A, then add B's "Phased GTM" slide before Metrics |

If two fit, take the one whose first section matches the prompt's first question.

## Frame shared by every template

**Open with two dark slides** (light is for content, dark frames the story):
1. **Question slide**: the prompt as one bold sentence, 1 to 2 key words in the accent color. Small kicker pill above ("Product Design Case"). Name and context at the bottom.
2. **Breakdown slide**: the prompt's sub-questions, numbered. If the user gave none, write 4 to 6 yourself. These become the tracker sections, one to one.

**Content slides**: each one is a dense poster (see design-system.md) with the tracker visible and a takeaway strip that states the point of the slide as a sentence, not a topic label. Tracker labels are short nouns ("Problem", "Users"); the strip carries the argument ("Every idle moment is now filled, so the mind never rests").

**Close with one of**: a key-moment statement slide (the one sentence the room should remember) and/or a dark "Thank you" slide. A short "Key point to note" box on the last poster is also fine (used in the Mars deck) when the user wants to flag scope limits.

**Where light slides go**: opening (2), one key-moment statement before the close, closing. Everything else is dense.

## Template A: Product build

Tracker: **Problem · Users · Solution · Risks & Mitigation · Metrics** (5 sections, 6 to 7 content slides)

| # | Slide | Takeaway strip says | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem | why the status quo is broken and why now | Hook quote; causal chain as 5 numbered steps; proposed-solution teaser (name, tagline, 3 to 4 feature icons); validation panel (donut + bar chart + evidence callouts); "why it matters" stat circles; one-line conclusion |
| 2 | Users | who feels it most | Segment cards (3); persona cards (3) with quote, pain points, needs, goals, solution line; user-journey table (stage / behaviour / challenge / opportunity); stakeholders in primary / secondary / tertiary tiers |
| 3 | Solution: concept | what the product is | Name + tagline; "how it works" flow (onboarding → home → 4 to 6 modules); onboarding Q&A table (what we ask / why it matters / outcome); results strip |
| 4 | Solution: experience | what the MVP does | Phone mockups with numbered callouts per screen; trade-offs table "intentional simplifications" (challenge / simplification / reason) |
| 5 | Risks & Mitigation | what could go wrong | Risk cards grouped (human and behavioural / technical and ethical); severity table (risk / severity / mitigation / monitoring); preventive safeguards row |
| 6 | Metrics | how success is measured | Metric table by category (adoption, activity, retention, reflection); North Star metric highlighted with a star; quantitative vs qualitative signals; "what success truly means" statement |

A single product often needs two Solution slides: one for the idea, one for the screens and trade-offs. Keep both under the same tracker label.

## Template B: Strategy / go-to-market

Tracker (adapt labels to the prompt's questions): **Problem Statement · Identification · Persona/Positioning · Go-to-market · Investment & Fundraising · Success Metrics & Challenges**

| # | Slide | Takeaway strip says | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem statement | which option wins and why | Options compared as a matrix (rows = options, columns = growth, market, longevity, impact, engagement) with a High / Moderate / Limited rating each; winner highlighted; 3 evidence callouts for the choice |
| 2 | Identification | how we find the right one | Process on a timeline arrow (e.g., 1 month / 2 months / 1 week) with a stage card under each; judging criteria as a pie or donut; perks row |
| 3 | Persona / positioning | what we build them into | 2x2 or 4-quadrant loop with a callout on each side; every callout carries a real-world example or stat in a dashed evidence box |
| 4 | Go-to-market | how it unfolds | 3-phase timeline arrow; one column per phase with 5 to 6 actions (key phrases bold); "step by step" staircase 1-2-3 (visibility, credibility, relevance); 2 to 3 real examples as side callouts |
| 5 | Investment & fundraising | what it costs and who pays | Cost tables per phase with totals; total in an outlined box; fundraising as 6 rounded bubbles; trade-offs list |
| 6 | Success metrics & challenges | how we know, what could break | 4 metric cards (name, formula, how measured, benchmark, target); pitfalls → mitigation as paired boxes with arrows |

## Template C: Market and unit economics

Tracker: **Research Insights · Unit Economics · Precision Demand Segmentation** (3 sections; the slides are the densest of all four templates)

| # | Slide | Takeaway strip says | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Research insights | what we learned and from whom | Primary research strip (survey n, interviews, FGDs); insights treemap or bar chart; secondary-research KPI tiles with sources; pain-point word cloud or graph; persona quote bubbles |
| 2 | Unit economics | does this work financially | Competitor radar (7 axes); opportunities list; consumer journey map (stages × actions, touchpoints, emotion line, pain points); metric gauges grouped in 4 categories; formulae table |
| 3 | Segmentation | where to start | Customer personas; 3-layer analytical framework (filter → behavioural segmentation → viability score) with a launch gate rule; cohort table (goals, habits, triggers, barriers) |

Close with a thank-you slide that says what happens next ("We're ready to turn insights into impact").

## Template D: Phased roadmap

Tracker: **Budget · Phase I · Phase II · … · Success Metrics** (bottom tracker works well here; the phases repeat the same layout)

| # | Slide | Takeaway strip says | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Budget / framing | the goal, the money, the pitch | OBJECTIVE strip; assumption panel (who you are, starting state); budget split across phases with big numbers; value propositions for funders as 4 icon rows |
| 2 to N | Phase slides | what this phase achieves | **Identical layout every time**: OBJECTIVE strip; key actions list each with an icon and a budget donut; "who is needed" icon row; problems row with matching solutions row below (columns aligned) |
| N+1 | Success metrics | how we measure, what could break | Metric list with icons; identified risks beside mitigation plan in matched rows; "Key point to note" box; thank you |

The repeated phase layout is the point: the reader learns it once and then only reads content. Change the content, never the geometry.

## Default flow for product cases (user preference)

**Problem → Who faces it (incl. pain-point prioritisation) → Solution / MVP → GTM → Metrics.** Use this order unless the prompt's own sub-questions dictate another.

## Frameworks (required)

Every content slide applies at least one named PM framework, **drawn as a graphic** (matrix, funnel, curve, tree, table with visual scoring), never as a bulleted list. Put the framework's name in a small tag on the panel ("RICE", "Kano") so evaluators see it was used deliberately.

| Stage | Frameworks to choose from | Graphic |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | 5 Whys, Fishbone, JTBD statement, cost-of-problem maths | Why-ladder or fishbone; JTBD sentence card; calculation tile marked Illustrative |
| Who faces it | Segmentation, personas, empathy map, pain points on frequency × severity, CIRCLES (Identify → Report → Cut) | Segment cards, persona cards, 2×2 pain matrix with the chosen quadrant tinted |
| Solution / MVP | RICE, Kano, MoSCoW, impact × effort, user flow | RICE table with score bars; Kano curve or 3-column Kano grouping; MoSCoW columns; flow diagram |
| GTM | TAM-SAM-SOM, beachhead, positioning map, Good-Better-Best pricing, phased launch | Concentric circles, 2×2 positioning, tier cards, timeline arrow |
| Metrics | AARRR, North Star metric tree, HEART, risk matrix | Funnel, metric tree, severity table |

**Ideate the graphic with the user.** Never render a framework as labelled text boxes (a list of "Why 1, Why 2" bars is still text). For each framework, propose 3–4 visual metaphors (iceberg, curve, funnel, tree, journey, gauge…) and **ask the user to choose before building**: use a question tool with previews where available. Ask every time graphics are being ideated, not just once per deck.

**Density bar is maximum.** 9–12 regions, ~85% of the canvas as content, 300–400 words, a graphic or sourced number in every region (see design-system.md "Dense layout spec"). When a slide feels light, add an evidence region (sourced stat rings, scorecard, mini-case) rather than enlarging existing ones, and offer to research sourced numbers (SKILL.md data policy). Fold small frameworks (e.g. JTBD) into an existing panel as a highlighted row instead of giving them a band.

Scores and ratings inside a framework (RICE numbers, Kano classes, matrix positions) are the user's judgement: draft them, label them "draft, adjust", and list them for the user to confirm. Market figures follow the data policy (placeholders or sourced).

CIRCLES can serve as the whole deck's spine: tag each slide with its CIRCLES step (Comprehend, Identify, Report, Cut, List, Evaluate, Summarise).

## Mixing templates

Interview-style product cases often need A plus pieces of B. Keep A's tracker and add an "Investment & trade-offs" or "Phased GTM" slide before Metrics; add its label to the tracker. Never exceed 6 tracker sections: beyond that labels get cramped and the story is too wide.

## Flow checks before you build

1. Read only tracker labels plus takeaway strips in order. Does it tell the story?
2. Does every sub-question in the breakdown slide map to exactly one tracker section?
3. Does each slide make one point, with every region on it supporting that point?
4. Does every problem have a matching solution and every risk a mitigation, visibly paired?
5. Are the numbers the user's? (See data policy in SKILL.md: placeholders, never invented.)
